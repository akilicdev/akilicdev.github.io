Ten Eras, One Map: The History Behind Atlas Dominion's Scenarios

Atlas Dominion lets you play in ten eras from 1200 to 2027. Here is a short, neutral introduction to the real history behind each historical starting year.

One of the things we enjoyed most while building Atlas Dominion was choosing its starting years. Each historical era captures the world at a turning point, and the map looks completely different from one to the next. Here is a short introduction to the real history behind each scenario. It is no substitute for a history book — but it might make your next campaign more meaningful.

A note before we start: the game simulates "what if" history. Once a campaign begins, events are fictional and depend on the players' decisions.

1200 — The age of sultanates and crusader states

Around 1200, Anatolia was largely ruled by the Seljuk Sultanate of Rum, while the Byzantine Empire still held Constantinople. In the Levant, crusader states existed alongside the Ayyubid realm founded by Saladin. Far to the east, the Mongol tribes were about to be united under Genghis Khan — a change that would reshape Asia within a few decades. It is a world of many small powers, which makes it ideal for players who like to start small.

1300 — The beyliks of Anatolia

By 1300, Seljuk power in Anatolia had faded and the region was divided into many small principalities known as beyliks. One of them, led by Osman, would grow into the Ottoman Empire. Elsewhere, the Mongol successor states still dominated large parts of Asia. A great scenario for an "underdog becomes empire" campaign.

1350 — A world after the plague

In the mid-fourteenth century the Black Death swept across Asia, the Middle East and Europe, killing a very large share of the population. England and France were early in what later became known as the Hundred Years' War. The Ottomans were beginning to expand into the Balkans. Population and stability matter a great deal in this era.

1400 — Empires on the move

Around 1400, Timur (Tamerlane) had built a vast empire across Central Asia and Iran and was on the verge of clashing with the Ottomans, which he did at the Battle of Ankara in 1402. In China, the Ming dynasty ruled; in Europe, kingdoms were consolidating their power. A scenario full of ambitious rivals.

1650 — After the Thirty Years' War

In 1648 the Peace of Westphalia ended the Thirty Years' War and shaped the idea of sovereign states in Europe. The Ottoman Empire, Safavid Persia and Mughal India were major powers, and the Qing dynasty had recently taken Beijing and was extending its rule over China. Overseas trade and colonies were expanding rapidly.

1800 — Revolutions and Napoleon

At the turn of the nineteenth century, the French Revolution had transformed Europe and Napoleon Bonaparte was rising to power. Britain was a leading naval and industrial power, and the young United States was growing. In this era, technology and economy start to decide who leads the world.

1914 — The eve of the First World War

In 1914, Europe was divided into rival alliance systems. The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in June set off the First World War, which drew in the great empires of the time: Britain, France, Russia, Germany, Austria-Hungary and the Ottoman Empire. Alliances are everything in this scenario.

1919 — A world redrawn

After the First World War, several empires had collapsed and new borders were being negotiated. In Anatolia, the Turkish War of Independence began in 1919 and would lead to the founding of the Republic of Türkiye in 1923. Across the world, new states emerged. It is a scenario about rebuilding — and about who will shape the new order.

1939 — The start of the Second World War

In September 1939, Germany invaded Poland, and Britain and France declared war, marking the start of the Second World War in Europe. Industry, technology and alliances determine everything in this demanding scenario.

2027 — A fictional modern world

The 2027 scenario is set in the near future and is entirely fictional. It uses today's countries as a starting point and adds modern systems such as cyber operations and a space programme with satellites and a mission to the Moon. It is the most complex era in the game, with the largest economies and the longest research trees.

Which era should you start with?

  • New to strategy games? Try 1914 or 1650 — clear rivals, manageable armies.
  • Like building from nothing? Pick 1200 or 1300 and grow a small realm into an empire.
  • Want the biggest challenge? 1939 and 2027 reward careful planning of economy, technology and alliances.

This article gives a brief, simplified overview for players. Historical events are complex; we encourage readers to explore them further in reliable history sources.
