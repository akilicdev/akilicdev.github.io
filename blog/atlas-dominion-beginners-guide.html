Atlas Dominion: A Beginner's Guide to Your First Campaign

New to grand strategy? This guide walks you through your first campaign in Atlas Dominion: choosing a country, building your economy, using diplomacy and fighting your first war.

Atlas Dominion puts the whole world on your screen: more than 190 countries, thousands of cities and ten different eras. That freedom is exciting — and a little overwhelming the first time. This guide will help you through your first campaign, from picking a country to signing your first peace treaty. You can follow along by opening Atlas Dominion in your browser.

Step 1: Choose an era and a country

Start a new game and pick an era. For a first campaign we suggest one of the mid-sized historical scenarios, such as 1914 or 1650: there are clear rivals and alliances, but not too many units to manage.

Then tap the country you want to lead. Beginners do best with a medium-sized nation with a coastline and few hostile neighbours. Very small countries are fragile; very large ones have long borders to defend and many provinces to keep stable.

Step 2: Learn the top bar

The bar at the top of the screen is your command centre. It shows your treasury and income, and gives access to the main panels:

  • Wars — every conflict you are part of and how it is going.
  • Technology — research that unlocks better units, mining, industry and, in the modern era, a space programme.
  • Maps — switch between map views to see relations, resources and more.
  • Ranking — compare your economy, technology, population and prestige with the rest of the world.
  • Investment — spend money on your provinces.
  • News — what happened around the world last turn.

When you have made your decisions, press End Turn. Every country then acts, and the calendar moves forward.

Step 3: Build the economy first

The most common beginner mistake is declaring war on turn one. Wars cost money every turn, and an army without a strong economy behind it runs out of steam quickly. For your first ten turns, focus on:

  1. Taxes — higher taxes mean more income but less stability. Keep stability healthy; unstable provinces produce less and can cause trouble.
  2. Investments — farms, mines and industry raise long-term income. Resources such as gold, oil, grain, boron and diamonds can boost it further.
  3. Technology — pick a research path that matches your plan. Economic and mining technologies pay off early; military research matters once you are ready to expand.

Step 4: Make friends before enemies

Tap any country on the map to open its panel. Here you can see your relations and choose from diplomatic actions:

  • Send envoy — slowly improves relations.
  • Request alliance — allies can join your wars and protect you in theirs.
  • Non-aggression pact — a simple way to secure a border so you can focus elsewhere.
  • Request or grant access — lets armies move through another country's land.

A good rule: before you go to war on one border, make sure your other borders are covered by allies or pacts. Be careful, too — acting too aggressively can turn the rest of the world against you.

Step 5: Your first war

When your income is stable and your army is ready, choose a target carefully. Look at the map for a neighbour that is weaker than you and not protected by strong allies. Gather your forces near the border before you declare war, so you can strike quickly.

Once fighting starts, take cities one at a time and protect the provinces you capture — newly conquered land can be unstable. Watch the Wars panel: if the war drags on, its cost adds up every turn.

Step 6: Win the peace

Victory is not only on the battlefield. When the war is going your way, open the enemy's panel and start peace talks. At the peace table you can set your terms. Taking everything is tempting, but a reasonable peace lets you rebuild your economy and avoid making new enemies.

Five tips from our own playtests

  1. Never fight on two fronts in your first campaign.
  2. Keep a cash reserve — surprises such as epidemics or unrest cost money.
  3. Check the News panel every turn; it often warns you about rising threats.
  4. Use the Ranking panel to see who is growing fastest — they may be your biggest rival later.
  5. Try multiplayer with a friend: host a room, share the code and see who builds the stronger nation.

Ready? Start your first campaign now. And if you have questions or ideas, write to us at info@tugrion.com.
